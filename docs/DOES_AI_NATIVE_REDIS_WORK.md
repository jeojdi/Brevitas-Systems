## 1. The answer

**Partly — the mechanism is real, the business as briefed is not.** The core claim survives: one agent hit the cache another agent paid to write, 24/24 times, with 100% of the shared block recovered and a 66.17% cost saving under ceiling conditions (E2). But the money claims fail. Shaping requests for a customer who already uses native caching measured **−30.84% on Anthropic** and −1.27% on DeepSeek (E1) — negative on both probed providers, with a measured steady-state ceiling of ~0.0%. The brief's headline — that determinism closes the ~5% vs 70–88% gap — is arithmetically impossible: the cross-agent ceiling at N=5 is **67.0%** even when 100% of every request is shared. And the ~5% field number the brief is built on turns out not to be a determinism story at all: 82.9% of it was measurement contamination, and the shipped sharing mechanism never actually executed (E4). Whether any of this is worth money now hangs on one number nobody has measured: the fraction of real customer traffic that is byte-identical, above-floor, front-loadable shared context.

## 3. The mechanism — why the numbers came out this way

Provider prefix caches are exact-match, position-keyed, and quantized (Anthropic: breakpoint-granular, 4,096-tok Haiku floor, 4-breakpoint cap; DeepSeek: automatic 128-tok blocks). Three consequences drove everything:

**Order beats bytes.** Matching starts at token 0. A per-agent system prompt in front of shared content caps the common prefix at 7–17 tokens on the repo's own corpora (E4) — below even DeepSeek's 128-block. Hoist the shared block to position 0 and the same content yields 66% (E2, live) or 90.2% of prompt tokens cached (the single-agent contrast case). Byte-canonicalization without reordering changes nothing.

**The arithmetic is fixed by the price sheet.** E2's measurement reduces to `saving = f × (N − 1.25 − 0.10(N−1))/N` where f is the shared-token fraction — at N=5, `0.670 × f`, model-invariant, verified live to 0.21pp. The ~5% field number corresponds to f ≈ 7.5%. The 70–88% single-agent numbers require f > 104% — impossible. Those come from a *growing* cached prefix across turns, a structurally different mechanism the cross-agent path cannot reproduce at any shared fraction.

**E2 and E4 agree on the villain and disagree on the corpse, and the disagreement is a finding.** E2's closed form says a real pipeline earning 5% would have f ≈ 7.5%. E4's receipts say the measured 5% was not produced by the mechanism at all: agents 2–5 recorded zero cross-agent cache reads in *both* arms; 82.9% of the headline was agent-1 arm-order inheritance on a shared key (both A/B arms, same credential, baseline first); and the shipped reorder never fired — `_reorder_safe` rejects any message list containing a system role, `dropin.py` never passes `pipeline=`, `_reorder_enabled()` defaults off, and the cold-start estimator poisons the threshold. So the brief's diagnosis (agents lose discounts to *nondeterminism*) is wrong twice over: the field yield to date is ~0 because of ordering, floors, and dead code — and DeepSeek reached 78.2% cross-agent hits with no help at all, directly contradicting the premise on automatic-cache providers. Determinism *does* matter mechanically — one top-positioned timestamp costs 12.15× (E3a) — but E3a measures the penalty, not its incidence, and nothing here measures how often real prefixes carry gratuitous top-positioned volatility.

**Why E1 went negative.** Both arms converge on nearly identical requests: a single tail-tracking breakpoint achieves incremental caching on Anthropic (the provider's documented pattern), so multi-breakpoint placement adds ~0. The −30.84% on top of that ceiling is a live defect: the ROI gate suppresses `cache_control` on every session's first call, which both skips the write *and forfeits a read of already-warm state* — $0.015082 of a $0.014556 arm gap (104%). Fixing it recovers the loss and creates no profit.

## 4. What this means for the product

**L1 — prefix compiler: split verdict; rebuild around ordering, drop placement as a value claim.** Placement/shaping over native caching is dead on both measured providers (−1.27%, −30.84%, ~0.0% ceiling). The levers that measured real money are *ordering* levers: hoisting shared context to position 0 (0.670×f at N=5, live) and relocating volatile tokens tailward (−91.7%/call, live). Neither currently works in the shipped product: the reorder is dead in four places, and on Anthropic it is architecturally impossible as designed — the wire order is tools→system→messages, so message-level reordering can never hoist content ahead of a per-agent system block; the doc must move *into* the system block. Concretely: (1) fix the ROI-gate bug now — it loses customer money on any short-session traffic today, independent of this thesis; (2) rebuild hoisting to operate on the system block; (3) resolve losslessness before selling it — `shared_prefix.py:9` says "strictly lossless" while `engine.py:262-266` stamps the same transform `response_faithful=False` / `experimental_unverified`; no output-equivalence eval exists. The floors are a hard product constraint: below 4,096 shared tokens on Haiku-class models the saving is exactly zero (measured), and the repo's own demo corpora are below floor on two of three providers.

**L2 — warming / TTL orchestration: keep flags off; the verdict stays negative.** The 2026-08-11 simulator showed warming loses to the 1h tier the engine already uses; E3b then removed the one cheap lever that could have changed that (0.1× reads cannot promote a 5m entry to 1h) and confirmed the incumbent is robust (reads cannot demote a 1h entry). A thin re-write niche survives only if reads do not refresh the 1h clock — unmeasured, probe specified below.

**L3 — tool/embedding cache: unsized, not validated; roadmap item, not a pitch line.** Zero evidence of demand exists: no tool-cache symbols, embeddings a pure passthrough, and the benchmark workloads disable the very tools that would generate the data. Do not call it "the biggest quiet win" on this record. The two content-free receipt columns E4 specifies (`tool_name` + salted `tool_args_digest`, `tool_result_tokens`) make it sizeable the day real traffic exists.

**L0 — content store / measurement plane: the strongest surviving asset.** Every decisive number in this run came off receipts. The one number the business now turns on — field f — is a query away once `warm_prefix_touch` (content-free digests + agent + hour bucket, the privacy shape `warm_prefixes` already uses) is added. Build the telemetry; it is the only path from "mechanism proven" to "worth money," and it is blocked today only by zero billable production usage since 2026-07-17.

**Positioning.** Drop "close the 70–88% gap" and any placement-based savings claim. What is honestly sellable: automatic hoisting + volatile-token relocation + provable receipts, quoted as `0.67 × measured shared fraction` at N=5 — with the caveat that a careful engineer can hand-build the layout, so the durable claim is *automatic and provable*, not *unavailable elsewhere*. Also honor E2's sub-prediction in sales conversations: a customer who turns on caching without reordering pays 24% *more* — "caching made my bill worse" is a real, demonstrable failure mode Brevitas fixes.
