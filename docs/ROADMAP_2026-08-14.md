## 1. The strategic frame

**PostHog's wedge was a measurement primitive; monetization came later.** Brevitas shipped a payout with
no measurement surface underneath it. That is the inversion to correct — and the demand evidence says
measurement is also what people are actually asking for.

**Ten-company study of beating "the customer already has the free version"** (GitHub/git, Datadog/Prometheus,
Stripe, Snowflake, Cloudflare, Confluent, Vercel, Algolia, Twilio, Mongo). Three patterns held 10/10:
1. **The wedge is always time-to-value. The moat is never time-to-value.**
2. **The moat always sits in a different layer than the wedge.** Nobody deepened their wedge into their moat.
3. **None of the ten charged a percentage of the savings they delivered. Zero.** All charged for capability held.

Only three durable moats existed: **M1** accumulated state nobody can backfill · **M2** cross-tenant
computation a single tenant cannot perform · **M3** legal control of the free thing.

**The graveyard is the warning.** Riverbed sold WAN optimization — "we make your bandwidth cheaper" —
peaked at a $3.6B take-private in 2014, **Chapter 11 in 2021**, killed by three simultaneous deflators:
input prices collapsed, the provider layer absorbed the function (SD-WAN), and traffic became *less
repetitive*. Our −74% retrieval result is that dedup-ratio collapse; provider-native caching is that
absorption. Cloudyn was absorbed and *became* Azure Cost Management. CloudHealth sold to VMware and
was folded in.

### We have no data network effect — ruled, with evidence from our own migration
`202608100003_warm_customer_state_hazard.sql:24-32`: *"no cross-organization behaviour is pooled into
it… customer → org → **a constant**. There is no fleet-level pooled model here **and none is planned**."*
The one place a network effect could have lived was consciously refused on privacy grounds.
Only three tables in the warm schema lack `organization_id`, and two of the three are our own probe rig.
**"A dataset obtainable for a dollar of API credit has zero N-dependence. That is not a moat; it is a
research budget."** (Our last two commits were $1.05 and $0.60 probes.)
The Sentry analogy fails on its own mechanism: Apple/Sony symbol resolution is a **cornered resource**
(a bilateral contract), not a network effect — customer #1 and #10,000 get identical benefit.

**Honest investor framing: we are not a network-effects business. We are a system of record with an
unbackfillable time series** — the Datadog/Sentry shape, where Coinbase spent ~5 engineer-years failing
to leave a $65M bill. Weaker claim, far more credible, and a sophisticated buyer will run the $1 probe
and catch the other one.

**Which makes the 13-month purge the most expensive line in the codebase.** `usage_log` deleted at
13 months, labels blanked at `202607280023:283`, **zero rollup tables exist**. We forfeit the only
uncopyable asset a little more every day.

## 4. Pricing

**The 1-3% band was folklore.** Real published evidence: **Datadog CCM 0.50% (Pro) / 1.00% (Enterprise)**;
CloudZero ~1.0% at $1M/yr decaying to **0.69%** at $10M. That's the entire public record. The folklore is
an artifact of banded flat fees — Vantage is 1.0% at a band ceiling but **2.7% at its floor**.

**The auditor split (this is the important part).** SOX's contingent-fee ban is narrow, but it surfaced a
worse problem: Rule 2-01(b) disqualifies anyone who **reviews their own work**. Brevitas *causes* the
optimization **and** computes the savings. *"Independent auditor" is factually false about our product today.*
ESCOs manage the identical conflict by **freezing the measurement method ex ante**, owned by a nonprofit
(IPMVP/EVO). **The ESCO is not the auditor; the protocol is.**

So split the roles:
- **Remediation** (we cause the change) → contingent is correct and industry-normal. Keep 25% on
  byte-provable levers, with ProsperOps' exact sentence: the *provider's own receipt* sets the number.
- **Measurement/attestation** (we certify) → **must not** be contingent. Flat, spend-tiered.
- **Reject % of spend audited** — aligns our revenue with the customer's waste, the precise conflict.
- **Reject % of identified savings** — that is Claritev: 2025 net loss $283M and a DOJ antitrust probe.

| Tier | Spend | Rate | Min |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free | ≤$5k/mo | 0% | — |
| Team | $5k-100k | 1.5% marginal | $299 |
| Business | $100k-500k | 1.0% marginal | $1,500 |
| Enterprise | >$500k | 0.5% marginal | $5,000 |

Seats free everywhere. **Hard cap: the fee never exceeds 25% of verified savings in the same period** —
this neutralizes *"you're paid to let me waste money"* completely, and no competitor can copy it because
none derive savings from the provider's receipt.
Blended 1.35% @ $50k/mo → 0.79% @ $1M/mo. 200 customers ≈ **$2.07M ARR**; 50 ≈ $517K. At 200, ten whales
are 34% of revenue and 140 Team accounts are 24% for 70% of support load — **the floor, not the
percentage, is the lever.**

**Meter the invoice, not the traffic.** Datadog does not observe traffic to bill %-of-spend; it ingests
the AWS CUR from S3. That is the only mechanism that works for Azure PTU and Bedrock provisioned
throughput, where **no per-token price exists at any point** — and it decouples billing from proxying.

**Migration is a ~3-week schema project, not a rate edit.** 25%→15% is constraint-safe (the ledger CHECK
is an upper bound; only CI fixtures break). A flat fee is **unrepresentable**: the `fee_microusd` CHECK
makes the fee a pure function of verified savings, so zero savings ⇒ ceiling 0 ⇒ INSERT rejected; there
is no `fee_kind` column; the period is CHECK-locked to 7 days; migrations are SHA-256 frozen; and
reconciliation requires an **exclusive meter writer**, so a platform fee must never touch
`brevitas_fee_microusd`. Give SKU A its own Stripe subscription, price, and meter.

- **Outcome receipts** (6-8d): a row for every request incl. non-2xx; `http_status`, `outcome`,
  `duration_ms`, `ttft_ms`, `stop_reason`, `attempt_ordinal`; `authoritative=false, fee=0`.
  TTFT is **not** first byte — hook `SSEUsageParser.feed`, stamp on first user-visible content.
- **Perpetual rollups** (8-10d): daily aggregates by (org, label-tuple, model, provider, strategy) that
  survive `retention.sh:69`. Cost columns in the read RPCs (`usage_grouped` whitelists 3 fields and
  returns **no cost columns at all**). An HTTP route for `list_usage_page` (exists, unreachable).
- **Label autodetect** (5-7d): persist `_agent_label` (computed at `proxy.py:1240`, thrown away); sniff
  `x-stainless-*` + UA for framework/gateway (both **empty on all traffic** today).

**P1 — THE WEDGE: Cache P&L.** Zero traffic, read-only admin key, **exact** signed counterfactual.
Both providers expose the full cached/fresh/cache-write split. **Derive unit prices from the customer's
own Cost API**, which makes it exact *and* absorbs their enterprise discounts, sidestepping our price
table entirely. Reconcile to the cent against their Cost API — that checksum is what makes it an audit.
*Build trap: Anthropic's `uncached_input_tokens` EXCLUDES cache read/creation; OpenAI's `input_tokens`
INCLUDES them. Same bug class as Luna.*

**P2 — THE "WHY": spend-change waterfall.** Decompose Δcost into volume / mix / price / prompt-size /
**cache-efficiency**. Requires per-call cached-vs-fresh splits **and** history — nobody else has both.

**P3 — The brake.** Budgets, spend alerts, killswitch. This is what the corpus actually asks for, and
being inline is a genuine structural advantage: a dashboard tells you that you overspent; only something
in the request path can stop it.

**P4 — Optimization referrals (advice, unbilled).** TTL tier, volatile-token relocation, GPT-5.6 fallback
removal, Fireworks replica affinity, geography tax. Give away what the spine forbids billing.

**P5 — Distribution.** PR comment + check run (**not** SARIF — code scanning is gated behind a paid
GitHub license on exactly the private repos our prospects have). OTel exporter. LiteLLM plugin **as a
read-only receipt collector only**, not a mutating hook.

## 6. Latency

`proxy.py:740`/`:1106` `await _emit_usage(...)` **before returning the response**. Root cause is worse
than a slow write: `api/store.py:7471` used module-level `requests.request(...)`, so there was **no
connection pool and a fresh TLS handshake per call** — and it is **two sequential calls** (`has_request`
GET, then insert POST). 2 × ~4 RTT is the whole 903ms. LiteLLM has the same problem and batches:
median 2ms / p99 13ms.

**DONE — pooled session.** `SupabaseUsageStore` now owns a keep-alive `requests.Session` with a mounted
`HTTPAdapter` (`BREVITAS_STORE_POOL_SIZE`, default 32), plus a `close()` for socket release. Measured
handshake cost removed: **~89 ms per call, and the receipt path makes two → ~177 ms per request**
(laptop → nearby HTTPS API; higher against a cross-region pooler). Pinned by
`test_supabase_store_reuses_one_pooled_connection`, which fails if anyone reverts to
`requests.request`. Three tests that patched the module function were moved to `requests.Session.request`.

**NOT DONE, deliberately — the `has_request` probe stays.** An agent proposed deleting it as a redundant
round trip. `api/server.py:5040-5052` carries an explicit comment that it **is** load-bearing (it is
authority-scoped, and it protects deployments where `202607280026` is unapplied). Do not trust the agent
over the code comment on a money path.

**NOT DONE, deliberately — no `BackgroundTask` shortcut.** Moving the write after the response with
Starlette's `BackgroundTask` would buy the remaining latency immediately, but it is a strictly *worse*
durability position than today: process death between response and task loses a billable receipt. That
is the exact trade this codebase has refused everywhere else. The durable queue is the real fix.

`BoundedUsageWriter` (`store.py:9183`) already has the flush thread, retry and dead-letter — its only
importer is a test, because it is in-memory and was not trusted with revenue. The durable queue is the
missing half: Redis Streams in-region, `appendfsync everysec`, fallback ladder Redis → SQLite WAL →
synchronous write → structured stderr. **Never fire-and-forget; when the queue is full, get slow.**
At-least-once is sufficient — the `(key_hash, request_id, authoritative)` unique index makes the insert
idempotent.

**Do NOT delete the `has_request` probe** as one agent proposed: `api/server.py:5040-5052` carries an
explicit comment that it **is** load-bearing, and it protects deployments where `202607280026` is unapplied.

**Fix `completed` first** (`proxy.py:1635`) — any p95 measured before it is survivorship-biased by
construction, since it excludes exactly the slow calls. **~10-12 engineer-days total.**

**The honest ceiling:** even at 5-10ms we lose to in-process Redis on cache hits forever. **Concede
exact-match response serving. Sell the miss path and the log path.**

## 7. `f` — measured, and mostly zero

Across 8 real multi-agent codebases, **f ≈ 0 as shipped in 8 of 8.** Every hand-authored shared prompt
measured 108-950 tokens; provider floors are 512-4096. **Hand-written shared context is structurally
sub-floor.** This retro-explains our 1.8% hedge-fund benchmark: haiku-4-5 (floor 4096) with a 1,972-token
shared snapshot — **structurally impossible, not a tuning failure.**

**Validity flag:** in every framework the shared block sits *behind* the per-agent block in the wire
format, so hoisting means moving text from `system` into a user message — not lossless. **The "66%,
24/24" replication almost certainly ran on a corpus where the shared block was already at position 0.
Re-check before that number is used again.**

The one lossless exception is the Anthropic `tools` block at true position 0, which requires
**homogeneous fan-out**. Threshold to matter: **f ≥ 0.35**. Zero OSS examples of the winning architecture
were found fully assembled.

## 8. Reliability — the gate that outranks every feature

**Provider degradation is measured, not theoretical.** Pulled live from Anthropic's status API, 32 days
to 2026-08-14: **37 API-affecting incidents (~1.2/day), 3,994 minutes = 8.7% of wall-clock degraded**;
major/critical alone 1,034 min = 2.24%. OpenAI in the same window: ~2 incidents touched the API.
**We are Anthropic-heavy, on the provider that degrades ~10x more often.**

- **We are a SECOND SPOF on top of the first.** `api/distributed_limits.py:3` — *"Production deliberately
  fails closed when Redis is unavailable."* A Redis blip in **our** infra 5xx's the customer's production.
  One region, 2 replicas, one Supabase, one Redis.
- `docs/PROVIDER_RELIABILITY.md:40` — *"Provider failures remain Brevitas-owned/SLA-eligible by default."*
  With 8.7% upstream degradation, **an unqualified 99.9% SLA is a breach machine.**
- **We cannot measure our own availability today** (no status/duration column; `completed` suppresses
  the receipt on every abort/5xx).
- **We already ship a zero-SPOF mode and don't sell it.** `brevitas.wrap(client)` mutates the body
  locally and the customer's own SDK calls the provider. **The SPOF exists only in the billable hosted
  mode. This is a pricing problem wearing an architecture costume.**
- Failover itself is commoditized and free (Cloudflare, Portkey, OpenRouter, LiteLLM all ship it).
  Do **not** sell it, and never let a cost-motivated model swap hide inside a reliability feature —
  a fallback's counterfactual is a 5xx; ours is a correct answer from the model they chose.
- Fail-open belongs in a ~150-LOC client shim keyed on an `X-Brevitas-Origin` header (so it never
  bypasses on a genuine provider 529), plus a server-side passthrough kill switch and a documented
  one-line rollback — which `ONBOARD_HOSTED_CUSTOMER.md` does not currently have.
  Bypassed calls produce no receipt and no fee: self-enforcing, and a bypass costs *us*, never them.

## 9. Governance — one design decision decides whether it works

**A hard budget stop must return 403, not 429.** The drop-in wrappers wrap the customer's own provider
SDK, and the OpenAI/Anthropic SDKs **honor `Retry-After` and retry automatically** — so a budget cutoff
delivered as 429 produces a retry storm against a wall, on exactly the traffic we are trying to stop.
403 with a distinct `detail` terminates in every drop-in client and matches the existing
`403 "Key lacks proxy:invoke scope"` precedent (`server.py:1949`).

Supporting facts: the limiter is genuinely strong (two-phase Lua, all 11 buckets validated before any
mutation, leases self-expire) — but `AuthContext` has **no spend/balance/entitlement field**, so there is
no plumbing for "over budget" to reach admission; `_provider_bucket` **discards the model string** (~2
lines to keep it, and the security audit already recommends this); `warm_customer_budget`'s reserve is
serialized by a **fleet-wide advisory lock**, fine for a batch loop and catastrophic per-request; the
lever kill switch is **replica-local**; and `/v1/jobs` bypasses the limiter entirely.
Budgets and a runaway circuit breaker share one prerequisite: a durable
`(organization_id, customer_id, lever)` trip table. Build it once.

## 11. Retention — free forever, and say so loudly

**Rollup storage scales with the customer's code structure, not their traffic.** ~2,000 distinct daily
label tuples for a 450-person org → **730k rows/yr/org ≈ 0.7 GB/yr**; 100 orgs ≈ 73 GB/yr ≈ **$9/mo**;
five years cumulative ≈ **$46/mo for the entire fleet**. A customer who 10x's spend costs us roughly the
same storage. That is what makes "free forever" a promise we can keep.

**Do not price retention.** Legitimate for Datadog (indexed, searchable, per-event); ours is a 200-byte
aggregate. Charging for the cheapest thing we own that produces the most lock-in destroys value — and it
contradicts the independence claim outright. *An auditor who deletes your evidence unless you pay is not
an auditor.* Langfuse caps at 3 years, Braintrust at 30 days; say ours is unlimited.

**GDPR: aggregates can legitimately survive erasure** (Recital 26 — the grain excludes every
natural-person identifier by construction, which is a *stronger* position than the ledger rows, which
survive only on the Art. 17(3) carve-out). Two fixes required in the same PR: collapse rows with
`calls < 5` into a `__lowvolume__` bucket at the 13-month boundary (k-anonymity floor), and a per-org
`label_retention` setting for the four free-text labels the customer controls.
Meanwhile `202607280023:283` currently blanks `provider`, `model`, `project`, `environment`, `source` —
**none of which relate to a natural person.** We destroy analytic value for zero compliance benefit and
still fail Art. 20 portability. **The worst of both.**

**FOCUS v1.5 ships December 2026 with AI support** — the working draft already carries
`ai_model_identity_examples.md`, with model identity on `SkuPriceDetails` and the input/output split
structural via `SkuMeter`. **It has no cache-read or cache-write meter. That gap is exactly our asset**,
and a proposal arrives with OTel cross-standard precedent rather than as a vendor's pet idea.
One person, ~6 weeks of calls, before December.

## 12. Provider absorption — 24-month survival scores

**The refined law, from reading 14 months of every provider changelog:** they absorb every **token
reducer** (compaction, context editing, memory, tool search, deferred MCP loading, effort/adaptive
thinking) *and* every **control** (spend caps, inference hooks, `--max-budget-usd`, model allowlists) —
control eagerly, because an uncapped runaway agent fleet is a chargeback and a churn event, so the cap
protects the provider. **What they refuse is accounting**: no arbitrary tag dimension, no per-request
cost row, no dollar figure they will stand behind. Anthropic's own `/usage` says its number *"doesn't
reflect promotional pricing or contracted discounts and may differ from your actual bill."*

> **Governance is dead. Auditing is not.**

`claude gateway` is far more than SSO: OIDC + per-group RBAC, multi-upstream failover, OTLP fan-out to
Datadog/Splunk/ClickHouse, 13-month spend retention, an audit trail, and a **spend-limit admin API**
(`/v1/organizations/spend_limits`, scopes user|group|org × daily|weekly|monthly, live 429, fail-open
toggle). Two exploitable facts: it **self-certifies as non-authoritative** (*"USD estimates, a circuit
breaker rather than an invoice; for billing, reconcile against your provider's usage reporting"*), and
**it makes caching worse** (*"1-hour cache TTL — Not available. The CLI omits the extended-cache-ttl
beta on gateway sessions"*). Anthropic shipped a gateway that degrades its own cache economics to buy
portability. First-party gateways are **single-vendor by construction** — that is the durable gap.
