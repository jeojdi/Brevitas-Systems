# Review: "Brevias — AI-Native Redis" brief, against Brevitas as it exists on 2026-08-11

Reviewed against: repo state on branch `warming-phase0` (15 commits / 82 files / 48,806 insertions ahead of `main`), live provider documentation fetched 2026-08-11, and the company's own measured cache maps. All provider claims below carry a source URL and retrieval date. All repo claims carry a file path and, where useful, line numbers.

---

## 1. Verdict

The brief is a competent greenfield plan for a company that does not exist. Brevitas is a shipped, deployed product, and four of the brief's seven phases are already built — several of them at a depth the brief never contemplates (the warming subsystem alone is 14 migrations, ~22k lines of SQL, and a 2,977-line replay simulator; the brief sizes it at "~1 week"). Its single load-bearing technical claim — that provider prefix caches are org-scoped — is wrong on the provider that matters most: Anthropic's first-party API isolates caches per **workspace** (platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching, retrieved 2026-08-11), and its most important proposed experiment (probe condition 5) is both mis-designed and ordered against a probe policy this repo has in writing. What the brief genuinely contributes is a short list of real gaps — the tool/embedding cache, the prefix-churn metric, latency measurement, the CI determinism test, semantic shadow-mode measurement — worth roughly two weeks, none of which is Phase 0. Adopt those, correct the provider facts, and discard the sequencing, the name, and the Redis-shaped product surface.

## 2. What's right and worth keeping

Be clear about this: the brief's best paragraphs are genuinely good, and two of them fix problems the company has not fixed itself.

**§1.2 — the determinism thesis.** "Cross-agent context sharing is mostly not an infrastructure problem — it's a determinism problem." This is correct, it is measured in this repo (a 5-agent A/B saved ~5% while single-agent multi-turn saved 70–88% — `token_efficiency_model/lossless/shared_prefix.py` docstring), and it is the bridge across the company's own live public contradiction: the committed site publishes `public/why-caches-dont-help.html` (":109 the cache is doing almost nothing") while the uncommitted whitepaper says native-cache recovery IS the product. §1.2 converts the published objection into the thesis: caches don't hit in multi-agent pipelines *because* of determinism, and making them hit is the product. Keep it verbatim.

**§4.2 — the prefix-churn metric.** Called "the single most diagnostic number in the system," and it is confirmed absent: `prefix_hash` is computed only on the warming path (`brevitas/warming.py:415,:463`), stamped only for warming-enabled orgs, and never compared call-to-call. `grep -rn churn` finds nothing but customer-churn text. This is the strongest single addition the brief makes. §9.3's churn alarm and §5.3's CI determinism test (no `test_determinism.py` exists among 85 test files) are its two companions and are also genuinely missing.

**Part 7 — the L3 tool/retrieval/embedding cache.** Completely absent from the codebase. `/v1/embeddings` is a pure passthrough (`brevitas/proxy.py:1901-1905`); zero tool-cache symbols anywhere. It is orthogonal to provider caching (a prompt cache never avoids an embedding call), the proxy already terminates the calls, and the tenant-namespaced encrypted store to key it into already exists (`brevitas/semantic_cache.py:222-250`). Highest value-per-line item in the brief. The hard rules (pure-tool allowlist, default deny, tenant scoping, negative-result caching with short TTL) are all correct.

**§8.2 — shadow mode and a measured false-hit rate.** The measurement half is right and missing: the shipped semantic layer has no shadow mode and no false-hit number, while `public/playground.html:838-844` already promises customers a quality gate. "Both numbers or neither" matches the company's own voice rule. (The *serving* half, 8.3, is a different story — see §3 and §5.)

**§12 — p50/p95 latency.** Right, missing, and urgent: no duration column exists in the ~60-column receipt schema (`api/store.py:1048-1110`), the Prometheus mirror publishes six counter families and zero histograms (`api/observability.py:87-100`), and the one measurement that exists is bad — p50 903ms / p95 2,084ms of Brevitas overhead on the cache-hit path against a customer's stated p95 <100ms bar (`docs/ENTERPRISE_SIMULATION.md:152`). "A cache that saves money and adds 300ms is often a bad trade" is the honest version of the unrefuted Redis objection in `docs/SHIP_READINESS.md:106`.

**§1.1 — the four-layer table and the KV-portability constraint.** Cross-provider KV non-portability is correct and confirmed by the prior-art survey (the real KV-reuse products — LMCache/Tensormesh — live inside self-hosted vLLM serving, a different market). Line 63 — "An ordinary cache is keyed on bytes you hand it. This one constructs the bytes so that somebody else's cache will hit" — is sharper than the whitepaper's current opening and should be lifted into it. One edit: drop the "lying or describing something else" clause; within-stack KV reuse is real and productized, and the accusation invites the counterexample.

**§9.2 — the thundering-herd concern.** Real, and confirmed by Anthropic's docs: "a cache entry only becomes available after the first response begins… wait for the first response before sending subsequent requests" (platform.claude.com prompt-caching, 2026-08-11). The company measured it live on 2026-07-02 (commit 79a5c3f): 6 concurrent Haiku requests over a 4.9K-token shared prefix cost $0.031698 ungrouped vs $0.008835 grouped (about −72%, with N−1 writes rather than N). The fix already shipped and is on by default (`token_efficiency_model/lossless/batch_group.py`, wired at `brevitas/proxy.py:104`, releases at TTFT on streams, fails open). So: right concern, already handled — what remains genuinely missing is the scheduler-hint verb for *predicted* bursts, and the untested DeepSeek arm (write-free caching makes the race there potentially larger, and it is independent of the "nothing to warm" conclusion).

**§1.4's honesty instruction.** "If an existing tool already does the differentiated part well… say so loudly rather than building anyway." Already the house style — this company shipped a −1.27% benchmark result (`benchmarks/native_cache_baseline_results_deepseek_n36.json`) and a whole-provider negative conclusion (`docs/DEEPSEEK_CACHE_MAP.md`). Keep the instruction; cite the precedents.

**§11.2 — context poisoning / taint.** Right, and it lands on a specific shipped module: `shared_prefix.py` auto-promotes content seen from ≥2 agents to position 0 with no provenance or taint field (`_PipelineState`, :64-76). Genuinely missing; see §6 for the concrete spec.

**Part 2, assumption 3** (input-vs-output split can kill the project) and **§10.4** (tenant ID fails loudly) are both correct instincts. 10.4 is partially unmet: the proxy and cache fall back to a credential-hash namespace rather than failing (`brevitas/proxy.py:637-650`, `brevitas/semantic_cache.py:329-331`).

### 3b. Right in general, wrong for this company

**"AI-Native Redis" as the name.** It names the product after its own unrefuted build-vs-buy objection: "A Redis exact-match layer is two engineer-weeks… with sub-millisecond hits instead of 903ms" (`docs/SHIP_READINESS.md:106`). It also collides with `docs/compliance/SLA_DRAFT.md:33` (Redis must never be treated as authority) while Redis Cloud sits on the subprocessor register. Keep the L0–L3 layer vocabulary internally; no "Redis" in any external artifact.

**§10.1 — the PUT/BUNDLE/RENDER command surface.** The shipped integration property is the absence of a command surface: "no SDK to adopt, no re-architecture… remove the base URL and you are back to talking to the provider directly" (`docs/brevitas-whitepaper.md:96`; pinned by `tests/hosted_snippet_carries_the_auth_header.test.mjs`; generated in five languages by `brevitas/cli.py:627-677`). PUT/BUNDLE/RENDER requires customers to stop constructing their own request bodies — the exact re-architecture the product promises to avoid — and `PUT <bytes>` turns in-flight bytes into a retained content corpus, colliding with `docs/compliance/RETENTION_AND_PRIVACY.md`'s binding schedule (raw prompts never persisted by default). Salvage two ideas as optional accelerators on the existing path: an `X-Brevitas-Prefix-Version` header (gives §5.2 rule 5 with zero re-architecture) and a scheduler-hint endpoint on the existing `/v1/warming` surface.

**§10.2/§10.3 — SDK-first, self-host-first.** Inverts the only topology that can produce an invoice. "The hosted proxy is the only path that produces billable savings… the local bvx install is architecturally unbillable" — `POST /v1/usage` forces `authoritative=False` as "a deliberate anti-forgery property, not a bug to fix later" (`docs/ONBOARD_HOSTED_CUSTOMER.md:9-24`; verified: `api/server.py:6716` is the only call site passing `authoritative=True`). The SDK already exists (`token_efficiency_model/lossless/dropin.py`) and was deliberately ordered second. The residency argument is answered in-house by BYOK request-scoped keys. If self-host must become commercially first-class, that is a *pricing* change (flat license) that reopens 10.6 — the brief has the dependency backwards.

**Part 15 — "adopt a gateway, don't build one."** Correct for a gateway as a routing product, wrong for this one as a **metering** product. Savings-share billing needs three things no adoptable gateway provides (verified from primary docs for LiteLLM, Portkey, Kong, Cloudflare, Helicone, 2026-08-11): both arms' provider receipts captured in-process on the same request path, a server-side point to randomize a control arm, and a per-request artifact defensible in a billing dispute. All five expose a hit/miss boolean and an estimated cost; none exposes a holdout or a billable receipt. The narrowed version *is* right: routing, retries, virtual keys, budget caps are commodity — stop adding surface there.

**§1.3 Key and Hit-miss rows.** "Content hash plus semantic neighbourhood" puts fuzzy matching in the primary key structure of a product whose fee rests on byte-for-byte losslessness. On the billable path there is no wrong-hit class; the actual posture is enforced structurally — everything content-touching is off by default and earns $0 (`api/server.py:4804-4814`). Split the table into "Lossless core" and "Quarantined levers, never billable."

**§5.2 rule 3 (sort retrieved docs by content hash).** The only §5.2 rule that mutates input semantics, with no correctness gate, in a document that elsewhere says a wrong hit is worse than a miss. It lands in the quality-affecting `reorder` class here and earns $0; the shipped retrieval module deliberately returns relevance order (`retrieval.py:556`). Delete, or rewrite as: "if the retrieved *set* is stable, hoist it into [B]; never reorder a per-query relevance ranking."

**§10.6/§17.1 — pricing as "open, no recommendation."** The 25% rate is enforced in three independent places (`api/server.py:4815`; `202607280007`'s fee function + CHECK + a self-check that RAISES on apply; `20260716_stripe_billing_rate_25pct.sql` against a verified live Stripe catalog) and published at `pricing.html:244`. The brief's stated objection — "hard to audit, customers dispute the baseline" — has a shipped, adversarially-reviewed answer it cannot see: anchored replays bill at min(list-price counterfactual, tokens_saved × the customer's own observed unit cost) with a written proof the fee ≤ 25% of what they would actually have paid (`202607280031:119-130`), plus the randomized holdout. Prior art strengthens rather than reopens the model: ProsperOps/Spendbase/Usage.ai/nOps bill 25–35% of realized savings in cloud FinOps; nobody applies it to LLM tokens. What *is* legitimately open about economics is covered in §5 and §8 below.

## 5. The load-bearing risks

The short list of things that, if wrong, invalidate large parts of any plan — the brief's or the company's.

**1. Anthropic cache scope is per-workspace, and the product's cross-agent story silently assumes co-workspace deployment.** Verified from two independent doc pages (2026-08-11). Consequences: (a) §1.2 must be rewritten as a per-provider/per-route table (Anthropic API = workspace; Anthropic on Bedrock/Vertex = org; OpenAI = org; Google = project; DeepSeek = same-key measured, cross-key unclaimed by policy); (b) probe condition 5 must become the three-arm design in §3a, under the standing provenance rule (a probe may only observe cache state a Brevitas-owned credential wrote); (c) onboarding needs a workspace-partition check — two keys claiming one org that MISS on a byte-identical prefix are in different workspaces, a named savings ceiling. The repo's holdout design survives intact: `warm_credentials` PK is `(organization_id, provider)` (`202607280001:28`), so one tenant = one credential = one workspace by construction; only the prose at `docs/WARMING_HOLDOUT.md:21-24` needs correcting ("scoped to the provider workspace the stored credential belongs to"). Beware the terminology collision: "workspace" in `api/` means Brevitas's tenant concept, not Anthropic's.

**2. The lossless invariant is one two-word edit from being breached.** `semantic_cache` sits in `_QUALITY_AFFECTING_STRATEGIES` (`api/server.py:4804-4807` — "cosine similarity does not prove answer equivalence") and therefore earns $0; promoting the string to `_BYTE_PRESERVING_STRATEGIES` would make cosine similarity a billing predicate. One test defends the line today (`tests/test_cloud_usage_api.py:557,:614-616`). Meanwhile `public/demo.html:381-382,:570` already sells "similar" and prediction while `playground.html:839` promises byte-identical replays — the contradiction is leaking in public. Action: add `test_semantic_cache_is_never_byte_preserving` asserting membership in both tuples; adopt 8.2's shadow measurement; refuse 8.3's serving path (or label it R&D for a future flat-fee SKU); rewrite demo.html.

**3. Provider auto-caching encroachment has no monitored number, and it has already partly happened.** All four majors now auto-cache by default in some form; free gateways do placement at zero markup (LiteLLM `enable_anthropic_prompt_caching`; Vercel `caching:'auto'` with `cache_anchor_items`).
