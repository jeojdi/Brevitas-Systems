# DECISION: None of the six clears the bar. Do not build any of them.

The per-recommendation fields you asked for — 90-day wedge, first five customers, price, prize, killer assumption — are deliberately empty. There is no recommendation to attach them to, and inventing one would repeat the exact error you paid for twice.

## The finding, which is not "they were all too small"

Three of the six actually cleared the size bar that killed all eight last round. C4's certification prize sized at $250M–$1.35B of revenue TAM. C3's category reaches $4.8B of premium by 2032. C6 sat on a real $1.5–2.5B existing budget line. Round one died on arithmetic; round two did not.

Round two died on a different thing, and it died on it six times out of six:

| | The control point is real | The asset that confers it |
|---|---|---|
| C1 capacity clearing house | allocation under scarcity | balance sheet + provider resale permission (contractually refused) |
| C2 agent-purchase underwriting | the approve/decline at checkout | loss reserves + 10 years of labelled chargebacks |
| C3 agent liability MGA | pre-execution gate + the policy | Lloyd's capacity + delegated authority + licensure |
| C4 insured certification | the procurement gate | accredited auditor + carrier paper + buyer recognition of the mark |
| C5 liability bureau | the form, the rating plan, the claim | statistical-agent designation in ~51 jurisdictions |
| C6 supervised agent conduct | pre-send interdiction in licensed activity | CCO relationships + examiner credibility |

Every durable position the search found is conferred, not built — by a regulator, a network, a capital base, or an installed customer relationship. You bring code. Code was the one input that was never scarce in any of the six. That is a consistent, six-for-six result, and it is what "control point" actually means in a market that is no longer new: the control was granted to someone, and grants are not discoverable by searching harder.

## Closing the loop the kill agents left open

Four of the six independently pointed at the same residual — nobody independently attests to *provider* behaviour: silent model swaps, quantization, capacity-driven routing, whether fast mode at exactly 2x delivers anything for the 2x. Three of them predicted it also dies. It does, and you should not spend a week finding out.

Size it the way you now know how, before designing it. The maximum recoverable value of proving a tier was not delivered is bounded by the provider's own credit regime — 10–40% of one month's affected fee, on a service-tier slice of the bill. On $10M of annual inference with 30% on premium tiers, the entire adversarial stake is a five-figure one-off credit. And Rule 1 fires from an unexpected direction: OpenRouter already publishes per-provider latency, throughput and uptime free, and Artificial Analysis already publishes independent quality-speed-price benchmarks free and is the de-facto reference. The independent measurement bureau for model behaviour exists and gives its output away, because measurement is a customer-acquisition cost for whoever monetizes the traffic. Independence is real here; the price of it is zero.

That closes the last open thread. Do not reopen it.

## Better or worse than the eight that died last round

Better as research, identical as outcome, and the difference matters.

The eight died because the asset was fixed and the buyer floated, so every one of them sized under 1% of a bill. These six started from sized budgets, and the sizing worked — it killed C1 and C5 in one step each, from a 10-K and a Form 990 that cost two fetches. That is a genuine methodological gain and you should keep it permanently: any candidate that cannot produce a defensible ceiling from public primaries in two fetches does not get a wedge, a moat, or a why-now written for it.

But the outcome is the same, and the failure has simply moved one layer deeper — from "wrong problem" to "wrong holder." That is a more informative place to be stuck and a worse place to keep searching from, because searching is the instrument that finds problems, and your binding constraint is no longer a problem you lack.

One thing did get established rather than refuted. The independence principle holds, sharpened twice: it prices only where the measured party is *adverse* to the measurement (C6's correction), and it points at attestation, never at inventory (C1's correction). In two rounds, exactly one structure satisfied both — insurance, where a risk-bearer pays for evidence about a party who would rather not be measured. That is why five of six candidates drifted into insurance without being asked to. It is also why they all died: insurance is gated by capital and licence, which is the one gate that venture equity is the wrong instrument to open.

## Are "stay in AI infrastructure" and "venture-scale" jointly satisfiable?

Yes — but not with the third constraint you have not stated and are actually enforcing.

AI infrastructure plus venture scale is obviously satisfiable; companies raise on it every month. What is not satisfiable, on this evidence, is: AI infrastructure + venture scale + **defensible at the moment of entry** + discoverable by a search process + reachable with code as the only asset. Twelve serious attempts, zero survivors, and the gating table above explains why that generalizes rather than being bad luck.

The fourth clause is the only one that is a preference rather than a fact. Venture-scale AI infrastructure in 2026 is not won by finding an undefended structural seat — six for six says there are none left undefended. It is won by distribution and speed into a window that stays open eighteen months, which is a bet on access and execution, not on structure. You have been searching for a position that is safe on arrival. That position does not exist, and the search cannot produce it.

## The one thing to do next

Stop running candidate rounds. Spend thirty days testing whether you can acquire one conferred asset rather than reason your way around needing one — concretely, whether you can become the technical half of a pair whose other half already holds capacity, paper, accreditation, a statistical-agent licence, or a compliance book. Three of the six kills independently arrived at that as the honest reading, which is the closest thing to a positive result in this entire file.

Falsifier and stop condition, fixed now: if after thirty days no such partner is reachable, the constraint to drop is venture scale, not the space. Dropping the space discards two years of earned domain judgement. Dropping venture scale only discards a preferred exit shape — and on this evidence it is the constraint the market is actually refusing you, not the industry.
