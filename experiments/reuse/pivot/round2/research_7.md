## Testing the independence principle

I ran this against live sources today. The principle as stated — *"a durable independent position exists where independence from the incumbent is the product"* — is **false in its general form**, and the failure mode is specific and recent enough to be decisive. What survives is a much narrower and more useful claim.

### What actually forced buyers to pay a third party

Across the analogues, four candidate forcing functions appear. Only two produced durable rents, and neither of them is "independence."

**Regulation that embeds the certificate in someone else's rules.** Credit ratings are the purest case. The SEC's 1975 NRSRO rules let institutions hold less capital against higher-rated bonds; pension funds and money market funds were legally barred from buying below thresholds. The result is measured: the Big Three hold ~94% share (S&P 50%, Moody's 31.7%, Fitch 12.5%), ran ~50% profit margins 2004–2009, and the 2008 collapse cost them **one percentage point of share, 98% to 97%**. Dodd-Frank explicitly ordered statutory references to ratings removed; implementation stalled and the mandate persists. Note what this proves: the agencies are **paid by the issuers they rate** and were catastrophically wrong, and the business was unharmed. Durability came from the mandate, not from being unconflicted. The same shape holds for audit — SOX §201 bans consulting for audit clients, §404 requires auditor attestation, the PCAOB inspects, and §906 carries 20 years' imprisonment. Independence there is a *statutory licensing condition*, not a value proposition a buyer chose.

**Liability borne by a third party with money at risk.** UL is the canonical case and the one most relevant here. It was founded in 1894 as a bureau of the **National Board of Fire Underwriters** — the insurers, who paid the losses, demanded the testing. It became effectively mandatory without a government mandate through OSHA NRTL recognition, electrical/building code adoption, retailer requirements and insurability. This is durable but the economics are an annuity, not a growth curve: UL Solutions is **$3.15B revenue, +6.4% YoY, 16.1% net margin, $15.3B cap, 30x P/E** after 130 years.

**Contract (a counterparty's procurement demands it).** SOC 2 and ISO 42001. Real volume, no rents: the attestation itself is commodity CPA work, and the value migrated to software that *prepares* you for the audit. The market's answer to AI assurance has already taken this shape — there is a **2026 Gartner Magic Quadrant for AI Governance Platforms**, and vendors like Holistic AI sell a governance platform, not audits.

**Adversarial interest alone.** This is the one the principle relies on, and it is the one that measurably fails. Two live 2026 data points: Integral Ad Science was **taken private by Novacap at ~$1.9B and delisted on 23 Dec 2025** after a roughly flat year; and on **6 Aug 2026 Nielsen agreed to acquire DoubleVerify for $13.60/share, ~$2.15B**, with DV's cap down ~21% YoY. The independent verifier of the platforms is being absorbed into the audience measurer it was supposed to be independent of. Their nominal standards body, the MRC, is a **seven-employee nonprofit funded by member dues that doesn't even perform audits** — it makes companies hire EY. Adversarial interest bought two ~$2B trade sales into decline, not a franchise.

**And two things that aren't independence businesses at all.** CROs are vendors, not verifiers — ICH is explicit that "the ultimate responsibility for the quality and integrity of the trial data always resides with the sponsor." IQVIA does $11B+ and Labcorp $14B doing *the work*; the genuinely independent roles (IRB, DSMB) are structurally tiny. Benchmark authorities have no forcing function whatsoever: MLCommons is a "community-driven and community-funded" association of 125+ members — i.e., funded by the vendors it measures — and is not a business.

### The capture mechanism, already documented in AI

The ad-verification failure has a mechanism: the measured party controls measurement access. The AI-native version has already been measured. *The Leaderboard Illusion* (arXiv:2504.20879) found Meta tested **27 private LLM variants** before the Llama-4 release with the right to retract bad scores; Google and OpenAI captured an estimated **19.2% and 20.4% of all arena data** versus **29.7% for 83 open-weight models combined**; and arena data access yielded **up to 112% relative performance gains** on the arena distribution. The most prominent "independent" AI evaluator was structurally captured within three years. Gray Swan, the leading red-team, lists Google DeepMind, OpenAI, Anthropic and Meta as clients — issuer-pays again. METR is explicit that "companies such as OpenAI, Anthropic, and xAI have provided access and tokens." Patronus AI, which launched as third-party LLM evaluation, has repositioned as a "frontier lab" selling simulation infrastructure on a $50M Series B — it left the independence position.

### Which forcing functions exist for AI providers as of today

**Regulation: present, and it was deliberately drafted to permit self-certification.** EU AI Act Article 43 requires a notified body only for Annex III **point 1** (biometrics), and even then only where harmonised standards weren't applied in full; **points 2–8 use Annex VI internal control, "which does not provide for the involvement of a notified body."** Article 55, the systemic-risk GPAI article, says providers "shall perform model evaluation… including conducting and documenting adversarial testing" — self-evaluation. California SB 53 (signed 29 Sept 2025) requires a published framework, transparency reports and 15-day incident reporting, and merely *encourages* third parties. The single US law that does mandate an independent AI auditor, NYC Local Law 144, is the measured failure: *Null Compliance* (arXiv:2406.01399) found **18 of 155 tracked employers posted audit reports and 13 posted transparency notices**, and Groves et al. (arXiv:2402.08101) found "wide disagreement over what constitutes a legitimate auditor" and concluded the law "has not effectively established an auditing regime."

The one genuine statutory independence hook is **AI Act Article 92**: the Commission "may decide to appoint independent experts to carry out evaluations on its behalf" and can compel "access to the general-purpose AI model concerned through APIs… including source code," with Article 101 penalties. That is real, unrevocable access — but the buyer is the European Commission. It is a procurement line item, not a market.

**Liability/insurance: the only forcing function actually forming, and it is the UL shape.** Armilla is a Lloyd's coverholder with capacity from Chaucer, AXIS, Convex, Swiss Re and Greenlight Re, and it explicitly couples "In-Depth AI Assessments" to an AI Performance Warranty — the assessment exists because the insurer eats the loss. AIUC sells the AIUC-1 standard with audits conducted by Schellman plus agent liability insurance, with ElevenLabs and Intercom named. This is 1894 Boston with different nouns.

**Adversarial interest: the substantive gap is real and provider-admitted.** Anthropic's own postmortem is as clean an instance as exists of an incumbent being unable to self-certify: three infrastructure bugs introduced 5/25/26 August, first user reports in early August, unresolved until mid-to-late September — **6+ weeks** — with **16% of Sonnet 4 requests affected on 31 August**, and the admission that "the evaluations we ran simply didn't capture the degradation users were reporting," compounded by privacy controls that "prevent engineers from examining the problematic interactions." Serving-level divergence is also already quantified: Artificial Analysis publishes an **Endpoint Accuracy Index** attributing score loss to "quantisation, sampling defaults, or other endpoint-side configuration." So the phenomenon is confirmed — and it is already measured **for free, publicly**. Rule 1 fires on the measurement primitive itself.

### Sizing before designing (Rule 2)

UK DSIT's economic assessment measured the AI assurance market at **£1.01bn GVA, 524 supplying firms of which only 84 are specialised, 12,572 employees, 60% SMEs**, forecast to **£6.53bn by 2035**, and states there are "currently no fully active certification schemes for AI assurance in the UK." That is roughly £2M of GVA per firm in a fragmented services market growing to a number a single mid-cap reaches. The fee layer is not venture-scale. UL's 6.4% growth is what winning this game looks like.

## Bottom line

- **The principle is false as stated.** Independence is never the product. In every durable case — ratings, audit, UL, notified bodies — independence is a *licensing condition* attached to a certificate that a third party with money at risk compels the buyer to obtain. Ratings and audit are both **issuer-pays and still durable**, which proves the rent came from the mandate, not from being unconflicted.
- **Adversarial interest alone is the failure quadrant, and it just failed twice on the tape.** IAS taken private at ~$1.9B (delisted 23 Dec 2025); DoubleVerify acquired by Nielsen at ~$2.15B (announced 6 Aug 2026), cap down ~21% YoY. The independent platform-verifiers were bought by the measurer. MRC has seven employees and no authority.
- **The capture already happened in AI.** 27 undisclosed Meta variants pre-Llama-4; ~40% of arena data to two providers vs 29.7% to 83 open models; up to 112% gains from that data. Gray Swan and METR are funded/accessed by the labs. Patronus abandoned the position entirely.
- **AI regulation was written to permit self-certification.** AI Act Art. 43 sends Annex III points 2–8 to internal control with no notified body; Art. 55 lets GPAI providers self-evaluate; SB 53 only *encourages* third parties. The one law that mandates an independent AI auditor (NYC LL 144) produced **18/155 posted audit reports** and no working definition of "auditor."
- **The single genuine forcing function that is emerging is insurance, not regulation.** Armilla (Lloyd's coverholder; Chaucer/AXIS/Convex/Swiss Re/Greenlight capacity) and AIUC (AIUC-1 + Schellman audits + agent liability cover) are reproducing the 1894 UL structure: the party that pays the loss demands the independent test. This is the only quadrant where the buyer of independence is not the incumbent.
- **The two hard tests any candidate must pass.** (1) **Unrevocable access** — every durable verifier had access it could not lose (statutory, as in AI Act Art. 92's compelled API/source access; or physical, as in UL buying the product at retail). Every fragile one had access granted by the measured party. In AI, the only unrevocable vantage point is the *buyer's own traffic*, not provider-granted access. (2) **Someone else's money at risk** — a certificate nobody is compelled to hold is a blog post.
- **Rule 1 fires on the measurement, not on the adjudication.** A provider will ship every eval, every endpoint-quality index, every trace viewer for free — Artificial Analysis' Endpoint Accuracy Index is already free, Braintrust is framework-agnostic and crowded, and Gartner already has a 2026 MQ for AI Governance Platforms. The one thing a provider structurally cannot ship is **an adverse finding about itself that carries a payout**. That asymmetry, and only that, is defensible.
- **Rule 2 verdict on the assurance/certification fee layer: it does not clear.** UK AI assurance = £1.01bn GVA across 524 firms, no active certification schemes, £6.53bn by 2035. UL Solutions after 130 years = $3.15B revenue, +6.4%, 16.1% net margin. Certification is an annuity. **The venture-scale version of this shape takes premium or transaction rent, not audit fees** — consistent with the OpenRouter/Portkey/Chronosphere pattern in the brief, where money accrued to the control point and the risk budget.
- **Unverified this round (flagged, not asserted):** the EU Digital Omnibus's status and any delay to high-risk obligations (all EC/Council fetches 403/404 — the AI Act timeline site still shows the remainder applying from 2 Aug 2026 except Art. 6(1)); SR 11-7 / OCC 2011-12 independent-model-validation text (Fed and OCC URLs 404'd); Vanta/Drata valuations; Nielsen's MRC accreditation history; Munich Re Insure AI terms; AIUC's raise size.
